Step 4 — GLM: Poisson vs Negative Binomial (2-year dataset)
============================================================

Script  : src/4_model_glm.py
Input   : data/2years/3_daily_counts.csv
Outputs :
  data/2years/4_irr_table.csv
  data/2years/4_model_comparison.csv
  data/2years/4_residuals.png

Formula:
  stressed ~ week_number + C(day_of_week_name, Treatment('Monday'))
  - week_number      : continuous trend across 2 years (1-104)
  - day_of_week_name : weekly seasonality (Monday = reference)
  - day_number excluded: collinear with week_number

Overdispersion check:
  Mean = 100.27, Variance = 748.97, Ratio = 7.33 -> overdispersion confirmed

Model comparison:
  Poisson           AIC = 9092.50
  Negative Binomial AIC = 6590.29  <- Winner (delta AIC ~ 2500)
  Note: BIC not used — Poisson uses deviance-based BIC, NB uses LLF-based (incompatible)

IRR table (NB model):
  Term                    IRR     Significant
  Intercept (Mon, wk 0)   98.3    ***
  Tuesday                  1.023  No  (p=0.53)
  Wednesday                1.022  No  (p=0.54)
  Thursday                 0.977  No  (p=0.53)
  Friday                   0.893  **  (p=0.002)
  Saturday                 0.809  ***
  Sunday                   0.853  ***
  week_number              1.003  ***
  alpha                    1.057  ***

Key findings:
- Mon-Thu are not significantly different from each other
- Fri/Sat/Sun have 11-19% fewer stressed posts than Monday (weekend effect)
- Stress posts growing +0.3%/week -> +37% over 2 years
- alpha significant -> overdispersion real, NB justified

Step 5 — Time Series Forecasting: Prophet vs SARIMA
============================================================

Script  : src/5_forecast.py
Input   : data/2years/3_daily_counts.csv
Outputs :
  data/2years/5_cv_scores.csv
  data/2years/5_cv_summary.csv
  data/2years/5_cv_plot.png
  data/2years/5_final_forecast.csv
  data/2years/5_final_forecast.png

Models:
- Prophet (Facebook): yearly + weekly seasonality, multiplicative mode
- SARIMA(1,1,1)(1,1,1,7): statsmodels SARIMAX, seasonal period = 7 (weekly)
- ARIMA(1,1,1) tested first — produced flat forecasts (no seasonality), replaced by SARIMA

Validation strategy:
  Walk-forward validation with expanding window
  4 windows, horizon = 21 days (3 weeks), min_train = 180 days
  Split points evenly spaced: linspace(180, 684, 4) = [180, 348, 516, 684]

CV results:
  Window  Train end    Train days  Prophet MAE  SARIMA MAE
  1       2024-07-12   180         77.6         18.6
  2       2024-12-27   348         20.9         18.3
  3       2025-06-13   516         18.8         12.9
  4       2025-11-28   684         10.7         12.5   <- Prophet wins this window
  Mean                             32.01        15.57  <- SARIMA wins overall

Key findings:
- SARIMA wins overall (mean MAE 15.57 vs 32.01)
- Prophet Window 1 MAE=77.6 — needs at least 1 full year to learn yearly seasonality
- Prophet improves drastically with more data (77.6 -> 10.7 across windows)
- Prophet beats SARIMA on Window 4 (10.7 vs 12.5) — most recent, most data-rich window
- Conclusion: SARIMA more robust with limited data; Prophet competitive with full 2-year history

CLI args:
  python src/5_forecast.py
    --input       data/2years/3_daily_counts.csv
    --output-dir  data/2years
    --horizon     21          (forecast days per window, default 21)
    --windows     4           (number of walk-forward windows, default 4)
    --min-train   180         (min training days before first window, default 180)
    --window-type expanding   (expanding or sliding, default expanding)

Fixes applied to src/5_forecast.py:
- Replaced ARIMA with SARIMA(1,1,1)(1,1,1,7) for weekly seasonality
- SARIMA now reindexes train data to full daily date range before fitting
  (prevents gap-induced misalignment of weekly seasonal period=7)
- Prophet already handles gaps natively via ds date column
- Added --window-type arg: expanding (default) or sliding (fixed train size)
- Sliding window tested — worse for Prophet (needs long history); expanding recommended
